The Plum in Kyoto's First Cup of the Year

Kitano Tenmangū picks about two tonnes of plums from its own trees in June. Priests and shrine maidens dry them in the sun all summer. On 13 December the shrine starts handing them out, six to a paper packet, for one cup of tea on New Year's morning.

北野天満宮 · 大福梅 · 皇服茶Kamigyō, KyotoFacts checked November 2026

You may have been to Kitano Tenmangū (北野天満宮) for the plum blossom in February, or for the flea market on the 25th of a month. Both are in every Kyoto guide. What the guides skip is what happens to the plums after the blossom drops. They don't go to waste. The fruit becomes a New Year charm, and in December the shrine hands it out over the counter.

It is called Ōfuku-ume (大福梅): "great fortune plum". The packet costs ¥700 and holds about six dried, salted plums. Kyoto households drop one into hot water or tea on the morning of 1 January. The idea is to start the year with luck and good health. Making them takes the shrine half a year, and the work happens in the same grounds you walk through for the blossom.

Here is the trade-off. In December there is nothing in flower and nothing to photograph except a queue at an amulet counter. What you get is a reason to be in north-west Kyoto in the quiet week before the year-end crowds. You also take home something you can actually use on New Year's Day.

Half a year inside one packet採取 · 土用干し · 授与

The shrine's grounds hold about 1,500 plum trees of some 50 varieties. They are there because of the man Kitano enshrines, Sugawara no Michizane, a ninth-century scholar remembered for his love of plum trees. The shrine's own description of the year goes like this.

In early to mid June, staff spend about a week picking the fruit. Different shrine pages put the yield at around two to two and a half tonnes. The plums go straight into barrels with salt. From mid-July, once the rainy season has ended, they come out of the barrels and are laid on bamboo slats spread with straw mats to dry in the sun. The shrine says priests and shrine maidens all take part. The drying lasts about four weeks, until the fruit is completely dry. In those weeks, the shrine says, the sour-sweet smell carries across the whole precinct.

Then the plums go back into salt until late November. Only then are they counted out, about six at a time, laid with a sprig of urajiro (裏白), the fern used in New Year decorations, and wrapped in hōsho (奉書) paper.

When: 13 December (the shrine calls it 事始め, the start of New Year preparations) until around 25 December, while stock lasts. Kyoto City's listing for 2026 gives Sun 13 Dec – Fri 25 Dec, from 8:30.
Price: ¥700 a packet, about six plums.
Reality check: "until they run out" is the shrine's own wording, and the shrine gives no number. The shrine also posts the plums by mail (cash by registered post, Japanese form, applications from mid-June) if you cannot be there in December.

A cure from the year 951王服 → 大福

The shrine's account of the name goes back to the reign of Emperor Murakami, in 951 (天暦5年). That year an epidemic was spreading and the emperor fell ill. He drank a tea with this plum in it and, the story says, recovered at once. After that he drank it every New Year's Day, and it was called ōfuku written 王服, "the emperor's draught". Later the luckier characters 大福, "great fortune", were put on the same sound.

Across the city, a temple tells the same story from the other side. Rokuharamitsu-ji (六波羅蜜寺), east of the Kamo River, credits the monk Kūya (空也), who is said to have handed out tea with plum and kelp to the sick in that same epidemic, and to the emperor. The temple still serves this tea, which it writes as 皇服茶 (ōbukucha), to visitors in the first three days of January. Most of the many English pages about New Year ōbukucha start here. Hardly any of them mention that the plum you take home comes from the other end of town.

I can't tell you which version is older. Both are stories, told by institutions that each have a stake in them. What both agree on is what to do: one plum, in hot water or tea, first thing on New Year's Day.

What to do with it on 1 January元旦の初茶

The shrine's instructions are short. On the morning of New Year's Day, put a plum into plain hot water (白湯) or into the first tea of the year, and drink it. Rokuharamitsu-ji's tea adds a small knot of kelp, musubi-konbu (結び昆布). Kyoto tea shops sell ready-made ōbukucha blends around the new year, often with the same kelp, but check the shop you have in mind; I could not confirm 2026 stock for any one store.

These plums are dried until hard and then salted again. Expect something sharp and very salty, nothing like the soft honeyed umeboshi sold in gift boxes. One plum flavours one cup, so a packet of six goes a long way.

If the counter is crowded and you can't see the packets, ask: 大福梅はまだありますか (ōfuku-ume wa mada arimasu ka).

Around the shrine in December上七軒 · 終い天神

Right in front of Kitano's east gate is Kamishichiken (上七軒), one of Kyoto's geisha districts. On 1 December each year geiko and maiko serve tea there for the shrine's tea offering, the kencha-sai (献茶祭). By the 13th that is over, and on a December weekday morning the street is mostly empty. Walk it end to end; it takes ten minutes.

Then there is the 25th. Kitano holds a market on the 25th of every month, and the December one, Shimai-Tenjin (終い天神), is the last of the year and the most crowded. It is also roughly the last day of the plums. If you want both, go on the 25th and accept the crowd. If you want only the plums, go on the 13th or soon after.

Getting there: Kyoto Station → city bus 50, about 35 minutes, to Kitano Tenmangū-mae (北野天満宮前). Or the Randen tram to Kitano-Hakubaichō Station (北野白梅町駅), about 5–7 minutes on foot.
When: the amulet office lists 9:00–19:30 on the shrine's site; Kyoto City's 2026 listing gives 8:30 for the first day. Check before you go.
Reality check: on the 25th, stalls cover the approach and the shrine's car park is closed. Take the bus or tram.

The other half of the day: Rokuharamitsu-ji in December空也踊躍念仏

You can't get the temple's tea in December. But Rokuharamitsu-ji has its own December rite with Kūya at the centre: the Kūya yuyaku nembutsu (空也踊躍念仏), a dancing recitation of the Buddha's name, performed in the main hall. It is listed as an Important Intangible Folk Cultural Property. In 2025 it was held on 13–15 and 28–30 December at 15:30, with the 31st closed to the public. I could not find the 2026 schedule. If it follows the same pattern, 13 December puts the first plum day and the first dance day on the same date.

When: 2026 dates not yet published as of mid-November. Check the temple's site or call 075-561-6980.
Reality check: this is a service in a small hall, not a show. Seating is limited, and photography inside a hall during a rite is generally not on.

The honest part

The object is small. You will queue, pay ¥700 and walk away with a paper packet the size of your palm. If you need a spectacle, come in February for the blossom or in November for the maples. In 2026 the autumn-leaf garden closes on 6 December, a week before the plums appear, so you cannot do both on one trip.

You will not see the plums being made. The picking is in June and the drying is from mid-July into August. If you are in Kyoto in that window, Kitano's grounds are where to smell it happening, but the shrine publishes no fixed schedule for visitors.

Check your own country's food rules before you carry dried salted plums home. Some customs authorities restrict plant products, and I can't tell you which rule will apply at your airport.

And the 25th is a crowd. The flea market is famous, the plums are nearly gone, and everyone in Kyoto who forgot to come earlier is there.

If you only have one day

Sunday 13 December 2026, from Kyoto Station. The afternoon depends on Rokuharamitsu-ji repeating its 2025 schedule; check before you go.

  1. 7:50City bus 50 from Kyoto Station toward Kitano Tenmangū-mae.
  2. 8:30At the amulet counter for the first day of the plums. Buy one packet, or two if you are sharing.
  3. 9:15Walk the grounds. Find the plum garden; the trees that made your packet are bare now.
  4. 10:00Out of the east gate and along Kamishichiken, end to end.
  5. 11:30Lunch near the shrine or around Kitano-Hakubaichō.
  6. 13:30Head to Rokuharamitsu-ji (bus or train; check a route app, allow about 45 minutes). See the temple and its statues while it is quiet.
  7. 15:30The dancing nembutsu, if it is on today. Arrive early for a seat.
  8. 16:30Done. If you are running late, drop Kamishichiken, not the counter at 8:30.

Then keep the packet sealed until 1 January.
