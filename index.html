Katakana is the first wall. シ and ツ, ソ and ン — they refuse to stay apart on paper. So stand up: the letters fall, and you catch the right one with your hands, your head, your feet. Your camera never sends anything anywhere.

Kanji Karuta — play against a friend

Karuta is how Japanese children learn to read fast: the reading is called out, and you race to grab the right card first. Here you hear a reading and swipe the correct kanji away with your hand. Send one link and you are playing someone on the other side of the world — no app, no sign-up. In English the readings show in romaji, so you can play before you can read.

Why this is not another textbook course

Three things you will not get from an app or a grammar book.

🕺

Rhythm, not memorising

I am a dancer. Verb forms, pitch accent and long vowels are rhythm problems, and rhythm is learned by moving — not by staring at a chart.

🩺

The Japanese of the body

I also work as a bodyworker. 肩がこる, 腰が痛い, だるい — the words you need at a clinic, a pharmacy or a job in care. Almost no course teaches them.

✨

Onomatopoeia, properly

ぴかぴか, どきどき, ふわっと. Japanese people use these all day and no dictionary can explain them. I can show them with my body until you feel the difference.
