Pheasants on the Gate: the On-matsuri Starts in Town

Most write-ups of Nara's great December festival begin on the 17th, with the costumed parade to the temporary shrine. Two days earlier, in the shopping streets off Sanjō-dōri, a lodging house hangs out pheasants, salmon and sea bream, and boils water to purify the people who will ride in it.

You have done the deer, Tōdai-ji and the lantern-lined path up to Kasuga Taisha. Maybe you even came back for the On-matsuri parade on 17 December, the one with the child horseback archers and the long line of Heian costumes walking down from the prefectural office. That parade is in every English festival calendar.

What is not in them is the 15th. The festival, now in its 891st year, runs from 15 to 18 December, and its first day happens away from the park, in the old merchant streets near Kintetsu Nara Station (近鉄奈良駅). It is short, local and slightly strange. This is a guide to that day, and to the dark night that follows it.

Why start with the 15ththe parade is the 17th; the people in it get ready on the 15th

The trade-off is simple. On the 17th you get spectacle and crowds, and the paid stands along the route sell out (seats run from ¥2,000 to ¥10,000 this year). On the 15th you get none of the spectacle, no seats, no English signage, and no published address for the main venue. What you get instead is the part of the festival that explains the rest: who used to pay for it, and how the riders were made fit to serve a god.

The lodging house大宿所 · Ōshukusho

The Ōshukusho is a retreat house. The festival's own programme describes it as the place where the Yamato samurai (大和士, Yamato-zamurai) who serve the horseback archery rites went into seclusion to purify themselves beforehand. The costumes and ritual gear for the parade are kept there, including the long field swords and the bamboo carried by the child riders.

Why would riders need a separate lodging in the town rather than at the shrine? Because the festival was paid for and staffed by the local warrior class of Yamato Province, not by the shrine alone. The lodging house was their base. The festival site says that in the old days the offerings came from the large and small lords of Yamato. Today they come from local worshippers. That is the reason for the strangest thing you will see on the 15th.

Pheasants, salmon, sea bream懸物 · kakemono

On the 15th, during the Ōshukusho rite, offerings called kakemono (懸物, "hung things") are hung and set out in rows in the front courtyard: pheasants, salmon, sea bream and more. The programme lists those three and adds "etc." Nothing is explained on site in English, and the courtyard is not a museum display. It is an offering to the young deity of Wakamiya, made in the shape of a pantry.

Read it as a list of what a Yamato lord could send: game birds, river fish, sea fish from a coast Nara does not have. A landlocked basin showing that it can command food from everywhere is the whole point.

The same evening, members of the local shopping streets serve noppe-jiru (のっぺ汁), a thick vegetable soup that the programme calls the festival's signature dish. Whether it is free, how many bowls there are and from what time is not published. Assume it runs out.

Hot water at dusk御湯立 · oyutate

Oyutate is a hot-water purification: a cauldron is boiled and the steam and spray are used to cleanse. At the Ōshukusho it is done three times on the 15th, at 14:30, 16:30 and 18:00. The festival schedule says the first is for the procession that arrived from the station, the second for the Yamato samurai in the old form, followed by the Ōshukusho rite at 17:00, and the third for general worshippers.

If you only catch one thing on the 15th, catch the 18:00 round. It is dark by then in December, and it is the one where standing there is the intended use.

Getting there: The festival site does not publish the Ōshukusho's address. The 13:00 procession leaves JR Nara Station (JR奈良駅) and walks down Sanjō-dōri (三条通り) into the shopping streets to the lodging house. Follow it.
When: Tue 15 December 2026. 13:00 procession, oyutate 14:30 / 16:30 / 18:00, Ōshukusho rite 17:00.
Reality check: No English, no seating, a small courtyard. How long the kakemono stay up after the 15th is not published.
If you lose the procession, ask in any shop along Sanjō-dōri: 大宿所はどちらですか (ōshukusho wa dochira desu ka). On this day the shopkeepers are part of the event.

The 16th: riders leave the lodging大和士宵宮詣 · Yamato-zamurai yoimiya-mōde

At 14:00 on the 16th, the Yamato samurai walk from the Ōshukusho up to the Wakamiya shrine. At 15:00 the dengaku troupe makes its own eve visit, and at 16:00 there is the eve rite. If the 15th showed you where the riders stay, the 16th shows you the lodging emptying out toward the hill. It is a quieter procession than the parade, on a weekday afternoon.

Getting there: It sets off from the Ōshukusho, so if you found it on the 15th, be there again before 14:00.
When: Wed 16 December 2026, 14:00.
Reality check: Exact route not published. Be at the start rather than guessing a point on the way.

Midnight in the dark遷幸の儀 · senkō no gi

At 00:00 as the 16th turns into the 17th, the deity of Wakamiya is carried from its main hall down to the temporary shrine (御旅所, otabisho) just inside the first torii. This is the core of the whole festival. The parade at noon exists to visit a god who has already been moved.

The approach is fully blacked out. The shrine's own text says participants may not take photographs and may not even switch on a torch. The 2026 tourism page repeats it: you may not light your own feet. No phone screens.

Where the public should stand is not published. Arrive well before midnight, dress for a December night in a park, and expect to follow the crowd in silence. A dawn rite follows at the temporary shrine from about 01:00 to 02:00.

When: 00:00, night of Wed 16 → Thu 17 December 2026.
Reality check: Cold, dark, long waits, no photos. Trains: if you are not staying in Nara, check your last train before you commit.

If you want the parade too

The 17th starts at noon from in front of the Nara Prefectural Office and goes past Kintetsu Nara Station, down Sanjō-dōri and up to the temporary shrine. Paid stands were on sale through the Nara City Tourism Association until 14 December: ¥2,000 in front of the prefectural office (about 11:30–12:30), ¥5,000 to ¥5,500 at the pine tree ceremony (about 13:00–14:30), and ¥10,000 in front of the temporary shrine (about 13:00–22:30). If the parade is called off for weather, the first two are cancelled. The rite at the temporary shrine runs from 14:30 into the night, and on the 18th there is sumo at 13:00 and noh at 14:00 on the grass stage there.

The Nara National Museum usually runs an On-matsuri display over the festival. A 2026 listing gives 12 December to 17 January; check the museum's site before you go, as dates and fees were not confirmed for this piece.

The honest part

The 15th is a small event in a small courtyard. You may spend an hour finding the procession, twenty minutes looking at hanging birds, and the rest of the afternoon waiting in the cold for a cauldron rite you cannot follow in any language. Nobody will explain anything to you. The soup may be gone.

The address problem is real. The shrine and the tourism association publish times but not the lodging-house location, so the plan below depends on catching the 13:00 start. Miss it and you are asking shopkeepers.

And the midnight move is not a photo opportunity. If the point of your trip is pictures, go on the 17th and buy a seat.

If you only have one day

Tuesday 15 December, based in Kyoto or Osaka, or better, staying the night in Nara.

  1. 12:30Arrive at JR Nara Station (JR奈良駅). Find the gathering near the station; the four shrine maidens and the procession set off at 13:00.
  2. 13:00Walk with the procession down Sanjō-dōri into the shopping streets to the Ōshukusho.
  3. 14:30First oyutate. Watch from the back; it is for the procession.
  4. 15:15Leave for the light: walk up Sanjō-dōri and look at the temporary shrine site inside the first torii, where the god will sit on the 17th.
  5. 16:30Back at the Ōshukusho for the second oyutate and the 17:00 rite. The kakemono are out.
  6. 17:30Noppe-jiru if there is any left.
  7. 18:00Third oyutate, the one for anyone.
  8. 19:00Dinner near Kintetsu Nara Station and back to your hotel.

Running late? Skip the 14:30 rite and the walk to the park. Be there from 16:30 to 18:30; that is the core.
