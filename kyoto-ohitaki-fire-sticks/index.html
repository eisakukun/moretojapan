Kyoto's Other Fires: the Ohitaki Fortnight

Fushimi Inari lit its fires on 8 November and the crowds went home. Across the city, ten smaller shrines and temples are still to burn their wish-sticks, with grilled mikan, steamed buns and a priestess spraying boiling water.

お火焚き ohitakiHigashiyama, Fushimi, Uzumasa and moreFacts checked 10 November 2026

If you have seen Fushimi Inari's fire festival, or read about it, you know the picture: three beds of flame in the sanctuary garden, priests feeding in bundles of wooden sticks, tens of thousands of them. That was 8 November at 13:00 (伏見稲荷大社, Fushimi Inari Taisha). It is the famous one, and it is already over this year.

It was never the only one. The Kyoto City Tourism Association lists fifteen ohitaki (お火焚き, "fire-burning") rites across the city in 2026, running from 5 to 23 November. Most are at neighbourhood shrines and a few at temples. From today, 10 November, ten are still ahead of you. This piece is about how to pick among them, why Kyoto ended up with this fire season at all, and what to eat on the way.

A fire season, not a fire festivalお火焚き · 火焚祭 hitaki-sai

The association's description is short: ohitaki has been held in mid-November since the Edo period, a fire lit before the deity to thank it for the autumn harvest and to ask for protection from bad luck. Nobody quite agrees on where it started. A family-oriented Japanese explainer gives three theories. One says it spread from the court's harvest rite, the niiname-sai (新嘗祭). Another says it grew out of thanking the hearth and tools that handle fire. The third is the winter solstice, the weakest sun of the year, when you light a fire to help it back. The same source notes that smiths held their own bellows festival (fuigo matsuri, 鞴祭) on the 8th of the old eleventh month, and that the two customs share the idea of thanking fire by burning something. Fushimi Inari's festival falls on the 8th. I would not read more into that than the source does.

What you will actually see is more regular than the theories. Visitors, or the shrine's parishioners, write a wish on a stick of wood (hitaki-gushi, 火焚串, or goma-ki, 護摩木). The sticks are stacked, the fire is lit, a purification prayer is chanted while they burn (ōharae, 大祓), and that is the core of it. After that every shrine adds its own thing. The shape of the stack is where they differ most.

Hanayama Inari (花山稲荷神社) is the one I wish had been a week later. Its stack is built in the shape of a bellows, because the shrine is tied to the swordsmith Sanjō Kokaji Munechika, who is said to have forged the sword Kogitsune-maru here under an Heian-period emperor's order with the shrine's god helping. The fire eats thrown-in mikan and the burnt ones are known as a cold remedy. It was 8 November at 14:30, so file it for next year. The same trick of the fire-grilled mikan comes back at one that is still ahead.

Saturday 14: Shin-Hiyoshi, a boiling-water rite新日吉神宮 · Imahiyoshi Jingū · 15:00

Shin-Hiyoshi Shrine (新日吉神宮, pronounced Imahie) sits on Higashiyama-dōri at the north end of Shichijō, beside Myōhōin (妙法院). You may well have walked past it on the way to the thirty-three-bay hall. The tourism association describes it as the guardian shrine of the retired emperor's palace. One local-history write-up places it at the northeast corner of the old Hōjūji compound, the "demon gate" direction, but I could not verify that, so treat it as a guide's story.

Its ohitaki is held every 14 November at 15:00. The order, per the listing, is a ceremony, village-style kagura, and then yudate kagura (湯立神楽): a cauldron of boiling water, and a priestess splashing it over people with a sprig of bamboo grass. Getting splashed is said to keep you well. Wish-sticks are burned. Fire-roasted mikan, said to keep you from catching a cold, are handed out, and so are bamboo-leaf lucky charms (fukusasa, 福笹) once the rite is done.

Getting there: Kyoto Station (京都駅) → city bus about 10 min to Higashiyama-Shichijō (東山七条) → 5 min on foot. The listing gives bus numbers 206 and 208 for the shrine's May festival; check which run on the day. When: Saturday 14 November, from 15:00. Free. No parking. Reality check: the boiling-water part is cancelled in rain. If the forecast is wet, call 075-561-3769 first, and use Japanese or a hotel desk.

Sunday 15: Gokonomiya, a quiet fire in Fushimi御香宮神社 · Gokonomiya Jinja · 16:00

This is the one I would pick if I had to pick one. Gokonomiya Shrine (御香宮神社) is in Fushimi, the sake district, a short walk from the station. The rite is on 15 November at 16:00. A pair of bundles of new-harvest straw is stood in front of the worship hall and lit with sacred fire (imibi, 忌火), and several thousand offered wish-sticks are burned with it. The prayers named in the listing are for the harvest and for the prosperity of industries that live by fire. A Rurubu listing puts last year's attendance at about 100. That is a listing number, not a count I can vouch for, but it suggests an evening where you will stand close to the fire and not behind a rope.

The shrine is more than the fire. Its name comes from gokō (御香), "fragrant": a spring is said to have started flowing in the grounds in the 860s, with a scent, and the story says the emperor of the time renamed the shrine for it. The water, Gokōsui (御香水), was picked as one of Japan's hundred famous waters in 1985, according to the sources I read, and counts among Fushimi's seven famous springs. I read that it dried up in the Meiji era and came back in the early 1980s. The main hall and the front gate are both Important Cultural Properties, and the gate is said to be from Fushimi Castle.

Getting there: from Kyoto Station (京都駅) take the Kintetsu line to Momoyama-Gōryō-mae (桃山御陵前), or the Keihan line to Fushimi-Momoyama (伏見桃山). Walk east on Ōtesuji. A Japanese listing instead gives JR Momoyama Station (桃山駅) at 5 minutes, so you have choices. Check the route in your own map app. When: Sunday 15 November, 16:00. Free. Reality check: finish time unknown. Dusk comes before 17:00 in mid-November, so the last part will be in the dark.

What to eat: the three-flame manjuお火焚饅頭 · ohitaki manjū

I searched for an English description of this and found nothing. In Japanese: during ohitaki season Kyoto confectioners sell small round steamed buns (manjū, 饅頭) with a brand mark of three flames in a row, a stylized flame-jewel (火炎宝珠). At the shrines it is the offering handed back to worshippers after the rite, together with mikan and a sweet called okoshi, and eating it is said to protect against illness and fire. Manzoku Inari Shrine (満足稲荷神社), whose own rite was on 8 November, listed red-and-white buns, triangular yuzu okoshi and mikan as its take-home set. I cannot name a specific shop that sells them this year, so look for the three flames in a wagashi shop window. Say お火焚饅頭はありますか (ohitaki manjū wa arimasu ka) and the person will know.

The rest of the list, by date11月16日–23日

If neither weekend works, the association's list gives you a week of options. All times are start times from the list.

Kyoto Ebisu Shrine (京都ゑびす神社), Mon 16, 14:00. The city's "Ebessan". They offer another boiling-water rite and burn the wood slips (hegi, 片木) with the names of parishioners, for health, safety at home and business.

Jōnangū (城南宮), Fri 20, 15:00. Several thousand wish-sticks, everyone present chants the purification prayer in unison, and the dance Urayasu no mai (浦安の舞) is offered. This is the weekday option.

Kōryūji (広隆寺), Sun 22. The 22nd is the monthly memorial day of Prince Shōtoku, and the temple's ohitaki is built around it. On this day only, it opens two normally hidden images: the Shōtoku statue wearing the imperial robe, and a Yakushi statue in the treasure hall. The temple is open 9:00–16:00 (treasure hall to 17:00). The hall service is at 13:00, and then tens of thousands of wish-sticks are burned in a fire-offering rite (goma, 護摩). It is in Uzumasa, the film-studio district; see our Uzumasa piece. Admission fee not confirmed, so check.

The 23rd, Monday holiday. Kenkun Shrine (建勲神社) enshrines Oda Nobunaga and lights its fire at the foot of the torii at 11:00. Shin-Kumano (新熊野神社) pairs a harvest rite with a mountain priests' fire rite at 11:00. Daishōgun Hachijin Shrine (大将軍八神社) is at 13:00. Hiraoka Hachimangū (平岡八幡宮) is at 14:00 and hands out sake and buns, with its monk-form deity statue possibly on free view from 10:00 to 16:00. Shiramine Shrine (白峯神宮), the sports shrine, burns its "wish skewers" (negai-gushi, 願い串) at 17:00 and gives out new white sake. Of the five, only Shiramine is a real dusk fire.

If you want to offer a stick yourself, go to the shrine office (shamusho, 社務所) before the rite and ask: まだ火焚串を納められますか (mada hitaki-gushi o osamerare masu ka). Some shrines take them in advance and some do not. One that does is Kurumazaki Shrine (車折神社), which takes them from July until just before its rite, according to the explainer I read, but I did not confirm its 2026 date.

The honest part

This is not a spectacle on the Fushimi Inari scale, and if you expect that, you will be let down. The fires at Shin-Hiyoshi and Gokonomiya are small, the audience is mostly parishioners, and nobody will explain what is going on. There is no English, and the end times are not printed. What you get is a few minutes of fire, a chant you cannot follow, and a mikan.

Two things I could not confirm: how long each rite lasts, and whether the manju is handed out to visitors at Shin-Hiyoshi or Gokonomiya (it clearly is at some shrines). Plan to arrive 20 minutes early and expect to be home before dinner.

Smoke clings to your clothes. You stand close to a real fire. The bigger risk is the weather: rain cancels the Shin-Hiyoshi water rite and may thin the crowd elsewhere. And the list I used is from the tourist association, which only lists the shrines that told it. There are likely more.

If you only have one day

Take Sunday 15 November. It is the one day on which the afternoon fits one clean line.

  1. 13:30Kyoto Station (京都駅) → Kintetsu line to Momoyama-Gōryō-mae (桃山御陵前). Check your own route app for the exact train.
  2. 14:30Walk Ōtesuji. Look in the wagashi shop windows for a three-flame stamp on a manju, and buy one to eat after the rite.
  3. 15:15Gokonomiya (御香宮神社). Look at the gate, then the spring, Gokōsui. Check the shrine's signs for the drawing hours; I did not confirm them.
  4. 15:45Stand near the worship hall, to one side. Do not stand in front of the priests.
  5. 16:00Straw bundles lit. Stay until the sticks are burning and the chant is going.
  6. 17:00Walk back to the station. Fushimi is a sake district, so if you have time left, look around near the station.

If you prefer Saturday, replace the first four lines with the bus from Kyoto Station to Higashiyama-Shichijō and be at Shin-Hiyoshi by 14:45. In the rain, switch to Sunday.
