The Night the Demons Dance Till Morning

Not the April Buddha’s-birthday Hanamatsuri. In the mountain town of Toei, a seven-hundred-year-old ritual runs through the dark in one small village hall after another, and the best part happens at dawn.

花祭 hanamatsuriToei Town 東栄町 · AichiDates checked 7 Nov 2026

You have done the autumn leaves at Kōrankei (香嵐渓) and you know what a crowded Nagoya weekend looks like. This is the opposite kind of November trip: you stand in a village hall or a shrine yard in the mountains from dusk until after sunrise, and the whole thing is free.

The festival is the Hanamatsuri (花祭) of the Okumikawa (奥三河) region, where Aichi, Shizuoka and Nagano meet. Each village holds its own night, on its own date, between November and January. The Aichi prefecture tourism site says the ritual is a designated national Important Intangible Folk Cultural Property, that it has been handed down since the Kamakura period, and that about forty kinds of dance are performed through the night. The Hanamatsuri Hall page says it has been handed down for 700 years. It is a Shinto ritual to drive off bad spirits and pray for the harvest and health. It is also, according to the same page, a night where the audience is expected to join in: the crowd shouts “tē-hoe, tē-hoe” (て～ほへ、てほへ) along with the dancers.

Eight nights, one valleyどの集落に行くか

The problem for a visitor is not finding a Hanamatsuri. It is choosing one. Toei Town lists a different village almost every weekend. In 2026 the run from mid-November to mid-December looks like this.

The villages are not copies of each other. The Aichi Now guide describes three “lines” (系) of Hanamatsuri in Toei, and gives a few differences you can actually watch for:

The two-in-one weekend is the one to plan around if you have a single trip and want a full night. Ashigome and Kōchi differ in the way that matters most to a visitor: Ashigome runs a full day and night in a community hall, while Kōchi is outdoors and starts at 18:00.

Ashigome: the dawn scene足込 · 明け方が見どころ

The Aichi Now text for Ashigome is unusually specific about when to be awake. It says the best part is the dawn, when many accompanying demons appear on the floor at once. A beehive (蜂の巣) hangs from the canopy over the dancing ground; once the demon Mokichi-oni (茂吉鬼) knocks it down, people go to pick up what falls, and whoever gets a piece is said to receive a small blessing.

Where: Ashigome Assembly Hall (足込集会所), Toei Town
When: Sat 28 Nov 8:00 → Sun 29 Nov 10:00 (the page says times can change)
Cost: Free. The page says that if you bring an offering (奉納金, hōnōkin) you get a local thank-you gift in return.
Parking: Yes, but expect crowding. The page gives about 40 minutes by car from the Hōraikyō (鳳来峡) exit of the Sanen-Nanshin Expressway or from Shin-Shiro (新城) on the Shin-Tomei.
Contact: Preservation society 0536-77-0022; Toei Town Board of Education 0536-76-0509.
At the door, say: 見学させてもらってもいいですか (kengaku sasete moratte mo ii desu ka). Entry is free; the question is a courtesy to a village that is hosting you in its own hall.

Kōchi: the same night, outdoors河内 · 長峯神社

If your idea of a festival is a fire and cold air, Kōchi is the other choice for 28–29 November. It is held in the grounds of Nagamine Shrine (長峯神社) and runs 18:00 → 9:00. The official description says that at dawn red, white and blue demons pierce a beehive hanging from the ceiling of the dancing ground with halberds, and that a coin falling from it, a “seed coin” (種銭, tanesen), kept in your wallet is said to mean you will not be short of money that year.

Where: Nagamine Shrine grounds (河内長峯神社境内), Toei Town
When: Sat 28 Nov 18:00 → Sun 29 Nov 9:00
Reality check: In late November, the mountains will be cold overnight. The pages do not mention seating or shelter; ask the town office (0536-76-0509) if there is somewhere to sit.

Before dark: the Hanamatsuri Hall花祭会館

On the Saturday, fill the afternoon at the town’s Hanamatsuri Hall (花祭会館). It shows, with masks, costumes, ritual tools, old documents and film, how the festival works, and it has a model of a dancing ground with costumed figures. It is open 8:30–16:30 and closes only from 29 December to 3 January. Entry is ¥320 for the Hanamatsuri Hall alone, or ¥330 each for the museum and the folk-craft hall (the prefecture lists them as separate tickets). The page does not say whether the combined tickets save money; ask at the desk.

Getting there, honestly行き方

Toei Station (東栄駅) is on the JR Iida Line (飯田線), and the town bus (町営バス東栄線) runs from there to Hongō (本郷), 10 minutes’ walk from the Hanamatsuri Hall, according to Aichi Now. I could not verify any train or bus times, either from Toyohashi or at night. The only shuttle-bus timetable I found for the festival was from 2019 and is out of date. For Ashigome, the official directions are all by car. Taxis are the practical answer for a night arrival or a dawn departure; call ahead.

The honest part正直なところ

This is not a show with a seat and a start time. Hours are long and listed loosely; the times on the pages can change. I found nothing about English-language help at the venues. You will be cold. You may not find food at the venue. You will not be able to get away at 3 a.m. without a taxi you arranged earlier, so decide before you go whether you are staying till morning. If you cannot stay up the night, Naka-shitara on 5–6 Dec runs from 13:00 on the Saturday, which at least starts in daylight.

One more thing: the year’s dates are on pages dated mid-October 2026. A neighbouring page listed 2025 dates for Kōchi (29–30 Nov), so the village pages are not all updated the same way. Phone the town office before you go.

If you only have one night一晩だけなら

Saturday 28 November. This assumes you have a car or a taxi booked; I have not verified a public-transport way to do it.

  1. 15:00Hanamatsuri Hall: about an hour. It closes at 16:30.
  2. 17:00Eat in town. I did not verify food at either venue.
  3. 18:00Kōchi, Nagamine Shrine: the festival starts. Bring the warmest layers you own.
  4. 0:00Stay, or move to Ashigome, which has been running since 8:00 the previous morning.
  5. dawnThe beehive scene at either place. Stay on your feet.
  6. 9:00Kōchi ends; Ashigome runs to 10:00.

If you are running late, drop the hall. Kōchi starts at 18:00 and Ashigome is already running, so you lose nothing but the museum.
