Who runs it

I am Eisaku — a native Japanese speaker, a breakdancer, a bodyworker, and someone who builds small strange apps. More to Japan is run by me alone. It is not a company, a school, or a tourism board, and nobody pays to be written about here.

The site opened in August 2026.

Who it is for

People who have already been to Japan a few times — or live here — and feel they are still standing just outside something. You have seen the famous places. You want the part that was never put into English.

The longer version is on the Why page.

How the articles are made

  • They start from Japanese sources — local histories, ward records, shrine and shop notices, Japanese-language writing that was never translated.
  • We check what already exists in English first. If a subject is already well covered, it is not written again here.
  • Places are never hidden. If somewhere is worth going, the article says exactly where it is.
  • No invented photographs of real places. Diagrams explain; they do not pretend to be the place.
  • Mistakes get fixed. If something here is wrong, tell me and it will be corrected.
